## Art-direction conclusion

The screen survives the transition to 3D without losing its loop. The most
promising direction is:

- fixed orthographic or weak-perspective gameplay camera;
- miniature handcrafted environment;
- rounded toy-like characters and chunky props;
- warm lacquered wood and amber lanterns against indigo rain;
- soft ambient occlusion and restrained depth of field;
- dimensional 2.5D UI using carved wood, enamel, parchment, and rubber;
- crisp icon readability despite real 3D lighting.

This uses broad qualities associated with premium chibi diorama adventures and
cozy creature life-sims. It does not copy any Nintendo, Pokémon, Zelda,
Pokopia, Link's Awakening, or Echoes of Wisdom character, logo, asset, icon,
interface, or world design.

## Useful discoveries

- The three comet motifs work well as warm sculpted magical lights.
- The front-facing service camera can coexist with miniature depth; the game
  does not need to become a fully isometric adventure.
- The original pixel screen already has enough depth layers for a real 3D set.
- The ingredient loop remains legible when food becomes tactile 3D geometry.
- Rain, wet reflections, steam, lantern flicker, and distant walkers would
  provide strong low-cost ambient motion.

## Deliberate non-final elements

- H3.1 was used only as a temporary anatomy/material reference.
- The top-right mood portrait drifted into a generic red face and must use the
  canonical Bentosaur face in a production mockup.
- The background dinosaur is exploratory and does not define cast anatomy.
- The rendered face is one delighted state; the production character still
  needs the planned replaceable expression system.
- The toon/pixel rendering choice remains open. This image tests full soft 3D.
